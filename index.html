What it is

A risk layer between an idea and an order.

Most trading accounts aren't lost to bad ideas — they're lost to position sizes that were too big, a losing day that kept going, or a rule broken in the moment. GlidePath exists to make those mistakes structurally impossible on my own accounts.

Strategies describe what they want to do. GlidePath alone decides whether and how much — sizing every order from its stop distance and a fixed fraction of equity, placing it with a protective stop, and refusing anything that would breach a limit.

How it works

Four stages, one direction of travel.

Signal

My own rule-based strategies emit a structured, authenticated alert: direction, entry, stop and targets. No sizing.

Risk gate

Every limit is checked first — drawdown, daily loss, losing streaks, correlated exposure, trade budget. Size comes from the stop.

Approval

By default I confirm each trade myself, with the exact size and money at risk in front of me, before anything is sent.

Execution

Orders go to my broker with the stop attached, then partials, break-even and trailing are managed and reconciled against the broker.

Principles

Rules that only ever reduce risk.

01

Every order is protected

The stop-loss lives at the broker from the first tick. If it can't be placed, the position is closed immediately.

02

Size follows the stop

Fixed-fractional sizing including costs, always rounded down. A wider stop means a smaller position — never more risk.

03

Days end on a limit

A daily loss limit, a stop after consecutive losses, and a profit cap end the session automatically. No exceptions.

04

No averaging into losers

There is no code path that adds size to a losing position. No martingale, no grid, no doubling down.

05

Friction is a feature

Locking the account is one click; unlocking needs a written reason and an enforced cool-down.

06

Broker truth wins

Positions are reconciled against the broker on a timer. Anything unexpected is flattened and the account locks.

Under the hood

Small, typed, tested.

A headless TypeScript engine with a dashboard client. The risk engine is a set of pure functions with a self-verifying test suite, including Monte Carlo runs on deliberately hostile sequences.
